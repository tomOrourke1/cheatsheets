Collision shapes and the axes between them

Hand-rolled narrow-phase tests for AABB, OBB, sphere, triangle and plane — the intersection predicates, the raycasts, the sweeps, and how to get a usable point, normal and t back out. Then the same jobs done with what Bevy ships. Everything assumes right-handed Y-up, unit ray directions, and f32.

Ground rules

Types and conventions

One shared vocabulary for the whole page. Every test below takes these by reference and returns either a bool, a contact, or an Option<Hit>. Ray directions are always unit length, so t is a distance in world units.

prelude.rs

glam re-exported by bevy_math

      

        On epsilons. EPS = 1e-5 suits scenes roughly in the 0.01–1000 unit range.
        If your world is in millimetres or kilometres, scale it. Absolute epsilons on dot products
        of large vectors are the usual source of "it works near the origin" bugs.
      

    

  


  
  

    
Axis-aligned

    
AABB

    

      Six floats, no rotation, and every test is a comparison or a clamp. This is the shape you use
      for the broad phase and for anything that never rotates. The one method worth internalising is
      closest_point — clamping a point into the box gives you sphere tests, distance
      queries and contact points for free.
    


    

      
Construct, query, grow

      

      

        Transforming an AABB means re-fitting it: rotate the 8 corners and take min/max, or
        use the absolute-matrix trick in OBB → enclosing AABB.
        Never rotate an AABB repeatedly — it grows every time. Rotate the source shape instead.
      

    


    

      
      
AABB vs AABB — test and penetration
O(1)

      

      

        The MTV lies at corners. Minimum-translation resolution snaps to a face axis, so a box
        clipping a corner gets pushed along X or Y, never diagonally. That is usually what you want for
        platformers and usually wrong for physics. For stacking, use a real solver.
      

    


    

      
AABB vs sphere, sphere vs sphere
closest-point

      

      

        Centre inside the box is the degenerate case: closest_point returns the
        centre itself, the delta is zero, and the normal is undefined. The code below falls back to
        the nearest face, which is the only sane answer.
      

    

  


  
  

    
Oriented

    
OBB

    

      An AABB plus a rotation. Point and sphere tests are just AABB tests done in the box's local
      space — rotate the world into the box, clamp, rotate back. Box-vs-box is the one that needs
      the separating axis theorem: 15 candidate axes, and finding one that separates proves no overlap.
    


    

      
Local space is the whole trick

      

      

        Non-uniform scale. An OBB has no scale field; bake scale into half. If you
        take the rotation from a Transform with non-uniform scale on a rotated parent, the
        result is a sheared box that an OBB cannot represent — decompose the global affine and accept
        the fit, or use a convex hull.
      

    


    

      
      
OBB vs OBB — SAT
15 axes

      

      

        Why the epsilon in abs_r. When two edges are parallel their cross product
        is the zero vector, and the corresponding axis test compares 0 > 0 with catastrophic
        cancellation. Adding EPS to the absolute rotation biases those tests toward
        "not separated", which is the safe direction: a false positive costs a wasted narrow-phase
        call, a false negative costs a tunnel.
      

      

        Getting depth out of SAT means tracking the smallest (ra + rb) - projection
        across all 15 axes instead of early-returning, then normalising that axis. Cross-product axes
        must be normalised before comparing depths, since they are not unit length.

Mesh soup

    
Triangle

    

      The shape your level geometry is actually made of. Two functions carry nearly all the weight:
      closest_point (Ericson's Voronoi-region version, which handles the seven cases of
      face, three edges and three vertices) and Möller–Trumbore for raycasts, further down.
    


    

      
Normal, area, barycentric

      

      

        Degenerate triangles are everywhere in imported meshes — zero area, duplicated verts,
        slivers. normalize() on a zero cross product yields NaN and the NaN propagates
        silently through every test that touches it. Filter at load time with
        area_sq < EPS * EPS, not at query time.
      

    


    

      
      
Closest point on a triangle
7 regions

      

      

        Use it for capsules too. Capsule vs triangle is "closest point between segment and
        triangle" compared against the radius; the segment version is the same case analysis with one
        more dimension. For a character controller, sphere-vs-triangle plus a vertical segment sweep
        gets you surprisingly far.
      

    


    

      
Triangle vs sphere · vs AABB · vs OBB
13 axes

      

      

        Triangle vs OBB has no separate implementation: transform the three vertices into the
        box's local space with obb.to_local, then run tri_vs_aabb against
        the box { min: -half, max: half }. Same for triangle vs any oriented volume.
      

    

  


  
  

    
Half-spaces

    
Plane

    

      A plane is a unit normal and a distance, and the only operation is a signed dot product. What
      makes it powerful is the projection radius: for any convex volume you can compute how
      far it extends along the normal, then compare one scalar. That single idea gives you box
      classification, frustum culling and sphere sweeps.
    


    

      
Representation and signed distance

      

      

        Two conventions exist and mixing them flips every sign in your codebase.
        Here n·x = d, so signed_distance = n·p − d. The other camp writes
        n·x + d = 0, where the same quantity is n·p + d. Bevy's
        InfinitePlane3d sidesteps this by storing only a normal and taking an origin
        point as a separate argument.
      

    


    

      
      
Sphere, AABB, OBB and triangle against a plane

      

      

        Frustum culling is six of these. Extract the planes with inward normals, reject the
        moment any test returns Behind. Testing an AABB against the near plane first
        rejects most of what a camera cannot see, for the cost of four dot products.
      

    

  


  
  

    
One dimension, many hits

    
Raycasting

    

      Each of these returns the same Hit: distance along the ray, the world point, an
      outward-facing surface normal flipped to oppose the ray, and whether the ray came from behind.
      Keep the return type uniform and callers stop caring which shape they hit.
    


    

      
Ray vs sphere
geometric, no divides

      

    


    

      
      
Ray vs AABB — slabs, with the face normal
branchless core

      

      

        The NaN case. If a direction component is exactly 0 and the origin lies exactly on that
        slab plane, 0 * inf produces NaN. glam's min/max forward
        to f32::min/max, which discard NaN in favour of the other operand, so
        the common path survives — but if you port this to raw arrays, handle it explicitly. Nudging
        zero components to ±1e-8 before recip() is the cheap fix.
      

    


    

      
Ray vs OBB · ray vs plane
transform, don't reimplement

      

    


    

      
Ray vs triangle — Möller–Trumbore
gives barycentrics

      

      

        The u, v are the point of the algorithm, not a side effect. They let you
        interpolate anything stored per-vertex — smooth normals, UVs, vertex colours, material blend
        weights — at the exact hit position. Weights are (1−u−v, u, v) for
        (a, b, c). Throwing them away means a second, redundant barycentric solve later.
      

    

  


  
  

    
The part that actually gets used

    
Getting information out of a hit

    

      A bool tells you a collision happened; a game needs to know where, which way, and
      how deep. These are the conversions between the four things every hit can produce.
    


    

      
From t to everything else

      

      

        Pick one normal convention and enforce it at the boundary. This page uses "the normal
        opposes the incoming ray" for raycasts and "the normal points from B toward A" for contacts,
        so a.translation += hit.normal * hit.depth always separates. Physics crates vary:
        check whether normal1 is expressed on the first or the second collider before you
        wire it into a resolver.
      

      

        Interpolated normals are not contact normals. A smooth-shaded normal is right for
        lighting and wrong for collision response — it will let a character slide along a wall that
        the geometric normal says is flat. Keep both: normal from the plane of the
        triangle, shading_normal from the barycentric blend.
      

    

  


  
  

    
Swept volumes

    
Sphere casting

    

      A sphere cast is a raycast against a shape that has been inflated by the sphere's radius — the
      Minkowski sum. That reframing solves it exactly for planes and spheres, and it is why the
      naive box version is subtly wrong: inflating a box gives you a
      rounded box, not a bigger box.
    


    

      
      
Sphere vs plane, sphere vs sphere
exact

      

    


    

      
Sphere vs AABB — expand, then refine the corner

      

      

        Two-phase is the practical shape of this. The expanded-box raycast is a cheap
        conservative bound: it never misses a real hit, it only over-reports near corners and edges.
        Refine those cases against the three edge capsules meeting at that corner and take the
        smallest t. If the hit lands on a face region, it is already exact.
      

    


    

      
Sphere vs triangle — plane, 3 edges, 3 vertices
character controllers

      

      

        This is the whole Minkowski sum, enumerated. Offsetting the face plane handles the
        interior, capsules along each edge handle the borders, spheres at each vertex handle the
        corners. Take the minimum t across all seven and you have an exact swept-sphere
        result — the core of every "move and slide" controller.
      

      

        Depenetrate before you sweep. If the sphere starts already touching, t = 0
        and the sweep tells you nothing about which way to go. Run a static
        tri_vs_sphere contact first, push out along its normal, then sweep the remainder
        of the motion.
      

    

  


  
  

    
bevy_math::bounding · bevy 0.19

    
What Bevy already gives you

    

      bevy_math::bounding ships AABBs, bounding spheres, raycasts and shape casts, with a
      uniform IntersectsVolume trait over all of them. It is deliberately a
      bounding-volume library: fast booleans and distances, no contact points, no OBB, no
      penetration depth. Everything above still applies for narrow phase.
    


    

      
Volumes and intersection
Aabb3d · BoundingSphere

      

      

        Note the two different Aabbs. bevy_math::bounding::Aabb3d
        is the one you compute with. bevy_render's Aabb component
        (centre + half-extents, in mesh local space) is what the renderer attaches for
        culling — useful as a source, but transform it before comparing against world-space volumes.
      

    


    

      
RayCast3d, AabbCast3d, BoundingSphereCast
returns Option<f32>

      

      

        These give you t and nothing else. There is no normal and no entity in the
        return value — reconstruct the point with ray.get_point(t) and the normal with
        the face-selection code from the AABB section, or with
        (point − sphere.center()).normalize() for spheres.
      

      

        RayCast3d stores Vec3A and Dir3A. The
        constructors take impl Into<_> so Vec3 and Dir3
        pass through fine, but reading cast.origin back gives you a Vec3A.
        Convert with Vec3::from(..) before mixing it into Transform maths.
      

    


    

      
Ray3d, InfinitePlane3d, and picking a mesh

      

      

        Mesh-level raycasting lives in bevy_picking as the MeshRayCast
        system parameter, which walks actual triangles and hands back a point, a normal, barycentric
        coordinates and the triangle index. Bevy's API surface here has moved between releases more
        than the bounding module has — check the docs for your exact version before copying signatures.
      

    


    

      
Draw everything, always
gizmos

      

      

        Collision bugs are visual bugs. An inverted normal, a stale transform or a
        half-extent used as a full extent are all obvious in one frame of gizmos and nearly invisible
        in a debugger. Draw the volume, the hit point, and the normal as a short ray from it.
      

    

  


  
  

    
When you want it done for you

    
Avian and Rapier

    

      Both wrap parry, so both give you the full narrow phase — convex shapes, exact
      shape casts, contact manifolds, a BVH over the whole scene. Reach for these the moment you need
      penetration depth against arbitrary geometry, or a shape cast that has to be exact at corners.
    


    

      
Spatial queries and their hit types
check versions

      

      

        These APIs move fast. Field and method names in physics crates change with almost every
        Bevy release, and the two crates disagree about naming (time_of_impact vs
        distance, normal1/normal2 vs one normal).
        Treat the block above as the shape of the API, not as a signature to paste.
      

    

  


  
  

    
Field notes

    
Things that will cost you an afternoon


    

      

        
SymptomCause and fix

        
          

            Hits register at half the real distance
            t is only a distance when the direction is unit length. A raw
              target − origin makes t ∈ [0,1] instead. Use Dir3,
              which cannot hold a non-normalised value.
          

          

            Fast objects pass through walls
            Discrete tests sample positions, not paths. Sweep the motion with a sphere cast, or
              cap the per-step distance below the thinnest collider.
          

          

            Rays hit the surface they started on
            Self-intersection at t ≈ 0. Start the ray at
              point + normal * 1e-3, or require t > EPS. Scale the offset
              with distance for long rays.
          

          

            Everything works until the object rotates
            An AABB was cached in local space and never re-fitted, or an OBB's
              rot came from Transform.rotation while center came
              from GlobalTransform. Read both from the same source, in the same frame.
          

          

            Boxes stick together at corners
            Minimum-translation resolution picks a face axis and pushes there. With gravity and two
              contacts, that alternates between axes and jitters. Resolve both contacts against one
              accumulated impulse, or use a solver.
          

          

            Rare NaN in the transform, never reproducible
            A degenerate triangle, a zero-length normalise, or 0 * inf in a slab test.
              Assert is_finite() on hit output in debug builds — NaN spreads far from
              where it was born.
          

          

            Sphere cast reports a hit in empty space
            An expanded AABB has square corners; the real Minkowski sum is rounded. Refine corner
              hits against edge capsules, or hand it to parry.
          

          

            Works near origin, drifts far from it
            Absolute epsilons against large coordinates. Compare relative to magnitude, or keep
              gameplay near the origin and shift the world instead.
